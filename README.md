# Main_Project
In the modern financial services industry, insurance fraud stands as a multi-billion-dollar challenge that impacts companies and consumers alike. When bad actors submit fabricated or exaggerated claims, insurance firms face massive financial leakages. To offset these losses, companies are forced to raise premium rates, meaning honest policyholders ultimately bear the financial burden. Historically, detecting these fraudulent activities relied on manual audits, retroactively spotting anomalies after a payout had already been disbursed. However, in an era of rapid digital transformation, traditional claims processing is being replaced by modern, proactive data analytics.
The system is designed to process historical claims datasets, identify suspicious behavior through anomaly detection, and visualize regional fraud hotspots. The project covers the full data lifecycle: extraction, database management, exploratory data analysis (EDA), machine learning, and business intelligence reporting.
This study utilizes a comprehensive dataset consisting of 100,000 synthetic automobile insurance records to evaluate systemic risk behaviours. The data replicates operational environments by incorporating 61 distinct attributes that capture the entire lifecycle of a claim—ranging from policy metrics and demographics to incident characteristics and historical risk profiles. Rather than evaluating these parameters in isolation, this analysis approaches fraud detection as a multivariate problem. By modelling the interactions between independent variables, we can successfully isolate the critical indicators that differentiate legitimate accidents from fraudulent submissions.
The objective of this research extends beyond descriptive visualization to the synthesis of actionable business intelligence. Isolating high-probability risk metrics allows operations to deploy an optimized workflow routing mechanism.
Modern insurance companies process thousands of claims daily, making it impossible for human adjusters to manually inspect every file for accuracy and potential fraud. Historically, identifying suspicious claims relied on retrospective manual audits, which are slow, inefficient, and often flag anomalies only after financial payouts have already been disbursed. This reactive approach results in billions of dollars in annual revenue leakage from fraudulent or exaggerated claims, forcing insurers to raise premium rates and pass the financial burden onto honest consumers.
However, modern insurance firms face a severe surge in sophisticated, fraudulent claims that routinely bypass traditional rule-based filtering systems. Analysis of the Insurance Claim Fraud Detection Dataset reveals a heavily imbalanced operational reality, where approximately 6% to 25% of historical claims are confirmed frauds. Relying entirely on manual investigation or rigid heuristics leaves the company exposed to a high rate of False Negatives (uncovered frauds), while simultaneously generating high False Positives that stall legitimate payouts and frustrate customer service divisions.
Core challenge of this project is to analyse 100,000 multi-dimensional insurance records containing 61 distinct attributes to proactively isolate high-risk portfolios without disrupting the user experience for legitimate policyholders. Failing to systematically detect these anomalies costs the insurance industry billions annually, lowering overall profitability and driving up premium prices across the board. The objective of this data analytics project is to utilize predictive machine learning algorithms on historical policyholder demographics, incident severity, and claim details. By doing so, this project aims to construct an automated binary classification model that accurately flags high-risk fraudulent claims in real-time, reducing financial leakage while accelerating approvals for genuine policyholders.
